# ENGTEST: engine and art-direction briefing

**Audience:** an agent that will turn this sheep-herding game into a top-down, isometric-style fantasy RPG / adventure / looter.
**Purpose of this document:** to explain *how this engine works* and *why it looks the way it does*, so that you can change what the game
*is* without breaking what makes it run and read well. It deliberately gives **no aesthetic instructions** (no palette, setting, creature
or UI re-skin guidance). The one look decision that is already made: **the final look is the engine's "32-bit" retro filter (`U.ret = 1`,
a PlayStation-style frame) applied over everything else** (section 6). Judge your work with that filter on.

All line numbers refer to `ENGTEST.html` in this repo (2,736 lines, about 414 KB) and will drift as you edit. Search for the
`// ---- ... ----` section headers instead of trusting a number.

> **Update: the conversion has happened.** The game is now **Quest** (section 17). The sheep game (sections 9 and 16's flock and pen
> hooks), the indoor night scene and its `NIGHT` composer (5.6), the cottage paint menu and the wardrobe are gone, and the day is no
> longer 720 s. Sections 1-15 still describe the engine correctly; where they describe the sheep game, read section 17 instead.

## 1. The engine in one page

- **One file, no build step.** `ENGTEST.html` holds the CSS, the HTML (HUD, menus, primer), an inline SVG icon sprite and **one
  `<script type="module">`** of about 2,300 very dense lines (it starts at line 423). Libraries come from jsDelivr through an import map (line 324): three.js
  **r185 WebGPU build** (`three`, `three/webgpu`, `three/tsl`, `three/addons/`), `three-mesh-bvh` 0.9.15, `meshoptimizer` 1.3.0
  (simplifier). Fonts come from Google Fonts. It needs a network connection (an offline single-file builder exists in the *other* repo,
  see section 14).
- **Renderer:** `THREE.WebGPURenderer` first, automatic **WebGL 2 fallback** (`?webgl` forces it). Every material is a **TSL node
  material** (three's shader graph language, JS that builds WGSL/GLSL), so one description runs on both back ends.
- **Everything is procedural.** There are **no models, textures, sprites, audio files or animation clips**. Terrain, trees, walls, ruins,
  the characters (signed-distance-field sculpts), grass (GPU compute), sky, clouds, weather, **sound effects and the music** are all
  generated by code at load or at run time. The only binary assets in the repo are eight JPEG screenshots in `tutorial/` for the how-to-play
  primer. Small textures (leaf atlas, straw, slate, flagstones...) are drawn into `<canvas>` elements at start-up.
- **Deterministic infinite-ish world.** Terrain height and every placed object come from **integer-hash value noise** that is identical
  in JS (physics, placement) and TSL (GPU grass, water, rain). The world streams in as **120 m chunks** built one at a time.
- **Camera:** a fixed-yaw, high three-quarter *perspective* camera (FOV 40) that follows the player (section 5.2). It is already close to
  the "top-down isometric" framing, but it is not orthographic and several systems rely on its fixed direction.
- **Gameplay is a thin layer on top.** The sheep game (flock AI, pen, wool economy, day/night, upgrades) sits in roughly lines 1820-2290
  and is separable from the engine in concept, though some world and shader code is hard-wired to the farm's coordinates (section 9.3).
- **Look:** a Breath-of-the-Wild-flavoured stylised renderer (soft cel lighting, wrap, translucency, rim, moving cloud shadows, wind
  everywhere, lots of grass) with **flat-faceted low-poly characters and trees**, a tilt-shift miniature-diorama post chain and a warm split-tone grade; and
  now the optional PlayStation-style retro pass at the very end.

## 4. The world

### 4.1 Coordinates and scale
Units are **metres**, +Y up. The farm is at the origin: the pen is a circle of radius 9 m (upgradable to about 22 m) centred on (0,0), the
cottage is at **(-21, -20)** turned 45 degrees. The shepherd is human scale (about 6.8 heads tall; collision capsule radius 0.45, height 2.4); a sheep's collision radius is 0.5.

### 4.2 Terrain
`H(x,z)` (line ~462) is three octaves of value noise times 36 m, minus 16, plus a slow sine, faded in between 38 m and 86 m from the origin (so the
farm sits on flat ground). `WL = -6.18` is the water level; ground below it is lake or stream, and a low-frequency mask `LKM` fills a share of the
lakes back in as hollows (the lake by the cottage is exempt). Two more functions matter:

- `Hm(x,z)` is the height of the **actual triangulated mesh** (not the analytic surface), so feet and grass sit on the drawn triangles.
- `HT`, `groundT`, `vnT`, `ihT` are line-for-line **TSL twins** of the JS noise, so the GPU grass, water and rain agree with the CPU.
  **If you change `H`, you must change `HT` identically.** The same applies to `puddleT` (it has a JS twin used for splashes).

Ground colour is computed per pixel from world position in `terrainColT` and the terrain material `TM` (noise blends between greens, a
yellow-meadow tint, sand near the waterline, packed earth for the yard and path). There are no terrain textures or vertex colours.

### 4.3 Chunks and streaming
- `CS = 120` m per chunk, `SN = 60` segments (40 on mobile), so 2 m (3 m) mesh cells. `mkC(cx,cz)` (line ~960) builds the terrain plane plus
  everything that stands on it, and its collider.
- **Placement is by hash, per chunk, and every chunk agrees.** A *field lattice* of jittered corners (line ~930) defines fields; each field's
  hash decides its walls or hedges, gates, lone trees, plantation, ruin, graveyard, hay bales, fairy fort, and so on. Each wall run, tree or
  bale is built by exactly one chunk, so nothing doubles or breaks at a seam.
- **Separate random streams.** Optional extras (logs, stumps, toadstools) use their own stream (`gcr`) so that adding or tuning them never
  shifts the layout of anything laid out before. Keep doing this: it is how the user's earlier world decisions stay stable.
- **Gameplay randomness** uses plain `Math.random` (aliased `R`); only world generation is hash-deterministic.
- **Streaming ahead** (line ~1199): because the camera looks one fixed way, chunks inside the view wedge and the heading wedge are built
  out to `AHD` (about 280 m) ahead of time, nearest first, so trees are in place before they come into view. Distant chunks are drawn lightly (small
  meshes drop out past `LVD = [150, 220]` m) and only chunks near the sun's shadow box cast shadows (`SHD`).
- **Instance pools** (line ~1159): a chunk keeps only instance data (matrices and colours per prop kind). One `InstancedMesh` per
  (geometry, material) holds every loaded chunk's instances and is repacked when chunks change state, so draw calls stay in the dozens.
  Any instanced mesh sharing a geometry and material with a pool **must be built the same way** (`IM`, `IMM`, `PoolAttr`), or three reuses the
  pipeline and reads wrong vertex buffers (the symptom was red/purple/green trees).
- A **far terrain ring** is rebuilt incrementally beyond the streamed chunks, hidden by fog.

## 5. Rendering

### 5.1 Pipeline summary
Scene pass (MRT: colour plus velocity) then the post chain, all as TSL nodes in an `EffectComposer` wrapper over three's `RenderPipeline`
(line ~2590). Pixel budget: the 3D is drawn at up to 2x (1.5x phones) but capped at about 3.2 million pixels (2.2 million on phones); DOM UI is separate and stays
crisp. Tone mapping is **AgX**, applied inside the chain (`renderOutput` in the split-tone step), not by the renderer's output transform.

### 5.2 Camera
- Perspective, **FOV 40**, near 1, far 1100. **Fixed yaw 45 degrees**: it sits at (+x, +z) of the player looking toward (-x, -z); "up the screen" is world (-x,-z).
- Zoom `ZM` runs 38-100 (mouse wheel / right stick), default 63. Camera distance is `ZM * 0.675` (about 26-68 m). **Pitch grows with zoom**
  from 0.48 rad (27.5 degrees) to 0.84 rad (48 degrees), so zooming out also tips the view more top-down.
- It looks at a point 1.5 m above the shepherd, easing toward it; the camera is lifted if it would dip near the ground or the line to the shepherd.
- Alternative cameras exist for the cottage menu (a three-quarter look at the house) and the wardrobe tab (head to toe), blended by `camU`.
- **Couplings to a fixed direction:** the look-ahead streaming wedge, the shadow box (centred 5.6 m ahead of the look point along the view),
  the grass focus (8 m ahead), the "sheep arrive off screen" logic, and the tilt-shift. They all assume the view direction never rotates.
- **See-through trees** (a technique you will want to keep in mind for this camera): tree materials discard (screen-door dither) fragments whose view ray
  comes down inside a ground disc around the player (`seeKeep`, materials `VWT`/`VLT`/`VBT`, uniforms `U.seeC/seeT/seeR/seeK`), so the player stays visible under canopies. Bushes are
  excluded on purpose.

### 5.3 Lighting and materials: "BotW look"
- One **directional sun** (shadow map 4096, 2048 on phones, PCF soft, box about +/-115 m that follows the camera's look point) plus a
  hemisphere light. Sun colour, direction, exposure and fog all come from the day/weather state each frame.
- `BotwMat` (line ~552) extends `MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial` with a custom lighting model (`BotwLighting`): a soft toon terminator (`toon`),
  half-Lambert **wrap**, **translucency** (`trans`, light through leaves), **rim** light (`rim`), reduced specular (`spec`), moving **cloud
  shadows** multiplied into the light (`CLOUD`), and wetness. Characters set `cel:1` for a hard two-tone terminator plus a toon highlight and flat
  rim band (Zelda-style). Per-material settings live in the `k` object; **`k.key` is part of the shader cache key**, so two materials with different node graphs need different keys.
- **Surface type is data.** Geometry carries a float attribute `aM` ("material id") whose meaning depends on the model class (for trees
  about 2 = leaf, 4-5 = bark, 6 = rock; for the shepherd 0 skin ... 9 tartan; for sheep wool/horn/hoof). Shaders read `aM` to add procedural bump, wind weight, wrap and
  specular. Vertex colours carry the base colour. Detail comes from procedural bump (`bumpN`, 3D value noise `nz3`) rather than texture maps.
- **Flat facets vs smooth:** props and legacy models call `computeVertexNormals` (smooth, facet-free); **trees, logs, stumps, the shepherd,
  the collie and the sheep are deliberately flat-shaded low-poly** (`flatN()` takes the true triangle normal from screen derivatives). This crisp
  faceting is a core part of the tree look. **The characters are now voxel models instead** (section 18): flat square faces, one colour each.

### 5.4 How models are made (there is no art pipeline, only code)
- **Simple props:** three primitives (`B` box, `Cy` cylinder, `Sp` sphere, lathe/extrude) merged by `mg([[geometry, colour], ...])` into one
  vertex-coloured mesh, `cvn` computes normals.
- **Organic models (trees, characters, sheep, rocks):** `SCULPT(S, ...)` (line ~636). You give a list of signed-distance primitives
  `[sdf, colour, blendRadius, materialId, bone]` (capsule `RC`, ellipsoid `EL`, triangle prism `TRI`, torus `TOR`, boxes, cones), smooth-min blended, optionally
  roughened by a noise function, sampled on a grid and meshed with **surface nets**. Options: `layer` (later primitives paint over earlier ones, giving crisp colour regions
  for cel shading), `ao` (baked cavity darkening), `bones` (bakes skin weights from bone ids), `dye` (bakes per-material weights so a shader can recolour regions live),
  `one` (keep the largest connected piece), `wind`.
- **`opt.lp`** (`lowP`) then **decimates** the fine surface to about N triangles with meshoptimizer, splitting vertices by colour region first so seams survive,
  gives every facet **one flat colour** (its region's) darkened by its corners' baked AO, and keeps rig weights per vertex so seams do not tear. This is how
  the "painted low-poly" look is produced. The whole shepherd is about 1,200 triangles.
- **Trees** (line ~744): a sculpted **wood** mesh (trunk and limbs, flat facets) plus **loose foliage cards** scattered through the crown volume
  (`foliage()`: alpha-tested leaf-card quads sampled from a 4-quadrant leaf atlas, thickest toward the outside, facing out along the SDF slope,
  darker deeper in). Species: oak, ash, hawthorn, Scots pine, Sitka spruce (with a darker solid core so plantations read dense), birch, yew, gorse, hedge. Each is a
  `[wood, leaf cards, collider radius, blossom cards]` row in `SPC`.
- **Wind** is a vertex-stage sway (`windOff()`) weighted by height and by `aM` (leaves move most, bark a little), driven by a uniform wind vector, so
  the whole country moves in one coherent breeze.

### 5.5 Grass, ground cover, weather, sky
- **Grass** (line ~1237): InstancedMesh tufts whose position, density, height, wind and trample are **simulated by WebGPU compute** (`instancedArray`, two layers). A
  jittered grid gives one tuft per cell inside a tile wrapped round a focus point, so it is an endless field; density and height come from meadow noise, farm masks, LOD fade
  and per-object clearings (`GCU`); a damped spring integrates wind gusts and kicks (the crook swing, footsteps). It is about 0.9 m tall, leans away from the camera so blades show their
  faces, and **hides low objects** unless a clearing is cut round them.
- **Sky:** a dome shader with a zenith/horizon gradient, procedural clouds and sun glow. **Fog:** `sc.fogNode` does aerial perspective (height-dependent) and a distance fade to the horizon colour; a separate **heavy fog** post pass clears about 8 m round the player and goes solid by about 25 m.
- **Weather** (line ~1820): a small state machine (fair, cloud, rain, fog, wind...) with minimum durations, wetness that soaks in and dries, **puddles** with screen-space reflections,
  rain streaks and splashes (stateless, animated in the vertex stage), wet footsteps and cottage lights. **Day length is 720 s** (12 minutes), with a sun arc, sundown, night and dawn.
- **Particles:** CPU-simulated into typed arrays and drawn as instanced sprites (dust, droplets, motes); chimney smoke is its own alpha-blended system.

### 5.6 The post chain (order matters)
`PuddleSSR` -> `GTAO` (half-res ambient occlusion) -> `MieGodRays` -> `UnrealBloom` -> `DoF` (a **tilt-shift** depth of field: a sharp horizontal band through the middle,
blur growing toward the top and bottom, plus real depth blur) -> `SMAA` -> `SplitTone` (AgX tone map, shadows pushed teal, highlights pushed warm gold, saturation, vignette) -> `HeavyFog` -> `TRAA`
(temporal AA **without** sub-pixel jitter, only to steady the frame) -> **`Retro`**.
The skinned characters write a fixed motion vector (`NOHIST`) so TRAA draws them fresh each frame (their pose is made in the vertex shader, which three's motion pass cannot see) and GTAO skips them.
A **second composer (`NIGHT`)** renders the night scene (the shepherd asleep by the fire, a second `THREE.Scene` built by `mkNI`) with bloom, a night grade and the same retro pass.

## 6. The retro "32-bit" filter (the target look)

It is the **last pass of both composers** (`retro`, line ~2606), switched by the uniform `U.ret` (0 off, 1 "32-bit", 2 "64-bit", 3 "PS2"). Switching is a uniform change, so there is no
shader rebuild. **Mode 1 is the intended final look.** What mode 1 does to the *finished* frame (after tone mapping, grade, fog and TAA):

1. **Resolution:** the frame is sampled on a grid of about **240 lines**: each retro pixel is an integer block of drawing-buffer pixels,
   `floor(height / 240)` on a side, sampled **point-sampled** (nearest) so the big pixels stay hard-edged.
2. **Colour depth:** every channel is cut to **5 bits (32 levels, 15-bit colour)**.
3. **Dither:** before quantising, a **4x4 ordered dither** table (`RDM[0]`, the PlayStation one, values -4..3 on a 0-255 scale) is added, which gives banded, cross-hatched gradients.

Facts that matter when you build on it:

- It runs on the already post-processed frame, so **bloom, AO, god rays, tilt-shift DoF and SMAA/TAA all happen at full resolution first** and are then downsampled and quantised. They are
  not authored at 240p.
- It is **session-only**: every launch starts at full resolution (a saved choice is deliberately deleted at start-up). Toggle: **R** or the pause menu's "Retro look". Currently the default is off.
- **DOM UI is not filtered** (HUD, menus, toasts are HTML over the canvas).
- Other places that mimic the filter by hand, which you must keep consistent if its parameters change: the **find popup's** 3D item picture (its own small renderer, drawn at the world's pixel size,
  5-bit dither and hard alpha cut, `fvRetro`) and the **music** (section 11).
- 64-bit and PS2 modes (bilinear blend with the N64 "magic square" dither; 448 lines point-sampled with a Bayer dither and a 1-2-1 line blend) are there for comparison; they are not the target.
- The screen-door dither used for the see-through trees uses screen-space interleaved-gradient noise; under the retro pass it is quantised with everything else.

## 7. Characters and animation

- **Update:** every character is now a **voxel model** (section 18). The rigs, IK and dye system described here still pose and colour them, but the knight's
  joints moved to his new proportions (section 18), and the shepherd-era numbers below (hip 1.52, shoulder 2.35, stride 2.2) are out of date.
- **No skeletal clips.** Characters are SDF sculpts with **bone weights baked per vertex** (`aJ` hip/knee/shoulder/elbow weights, `aH` head), posed **in the vertex shader (TSL)**
  by procedural rigs. The shepherd's gait is computed (stride-matched hip swing, knee fold, ankle roll, waist lean, spring-driven free arm and coat skirt, breathing and weight shift when idle). The
  walking staff plants ahead each stride; the crook swing is a scripted sweep (wind-up, eased sweep, follow-through) with **two-bone IK** putting the hand on the grip; there is a swing-trail ribbon.
- **Dye system:** `opt.dye` bakes per-region weights so the wardrobe and cottage menus can recolour coat, cap, trousers, wellies, hair and beard live without rebuilding the mesh
  (each region keeps the baked brightness so shading and AO survive). The shepherd also has a pyjama variant for the night scene (`PLMn`, `DGMn` reuse the rig).
- **The flock:** `MX = 180` pooled sheep records (`S`, no allocation in gameplay). Two LODs (a near one with glossy eyes) share a material and an instanced rig driven by per-instance attributes. Sheep are flat-faceted
  like the shepherd.
- **Hard WebGPU limit you will hit:** 8 vertex buffers per draw. The shepherd's baked attributes exceeded it, so named float attributes are packed into one interleaved buffer.
- **Collie:** a rigged dog with fetch behaviour (an upgrade).
- **A UI item model path exists:** `fvModel` builds hollow-garment 3D models for loot popups, rendered by a small second renderer (`fv3`), turning slowly over a soft shadow.

## 8. Collision and movement

- No physics engine; movement is **kinematic**. Static colliders are merged `three-mesh-bvh` meshes: `mkCol(geoms)` builds a BVH; `COLS = [FARMC, FENCEC, ...chunk colliders]`.
- `capsule(p, r, h)` pushes a vertical capsule out of every BVH it touches (it only registers **surfaces** within `r`, so probing from *inside* a large solid can read "free"; test by approaching from outside).
  `sweep(...)` sub-steps a move so nothing tunnels through thin rails, walls or trunks. The shepherd also gets **corner-slip** (a stalled step is retried turned 35 and 65 degrees).
- Colliders are authored in each object's own space and carried by the object's matrix (the pen furniture does this, so it scales with upgrades). Walls are flagged so dogs and stranded sheep can hop them. Gates are
  separate colliders (`GATES`) that the crook swings open.
- Vertical placement uses `Hm()`. Water is "shallow only" for the player (a step that ends too deep is projected back onto the ankle-depth contour so he slides along the shore).
- Sheep use `sweep(s, mx, mz, 0.5, 1.6)` within 90 m of the player; farther sheep just move.

## 9. Gameplay layer (the sheep game): what exists, where it lives

### 9.1 Systems
| System | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Player control | `mvP`, `camU`, gamepad, touch | WASD / arrows, Space = crook, F shop, C cottage, Esc pause, wheel zoom; touch = floating joystick + round crook button; standard gamepad with rumble |
| Sheep AI | `shp` (line ~2083) | states `st`: 0 hidden in a bush, 1 loose (wanders, bolts from the player inside 9 m), 2 follower (formation behind the player), 3 penned, 4 fleeing/escaped. Trail-following when a wall cuts a follower off, stuck rescue, water avoidance, hop over fences |
| Crook | `crookSweep` | a swing pulls every loose/hiding sheep in the arc into the flock, opens gates, knocks trunks open |
| Pen and economy | `deposit`, `UP`, `L` | wool `W`; upgrades (crook, pen, trough, hay, shelter, garden, boots, dog) with level tables; happiness `hp` from comforts; escape rules by happiness and by night |
| Day/night | `dayU`, `sundown`, `nightStart`, `NIGHT` | 720 s day; sundown, wolf howls, night escapes, a "counting sheep" dream sequence, dawn payout |
| Finds (loot) | `FIND`, `fndU`, `fpQ` | ruins, farmsteads and graveyards hold a shimmering trunk; the crook opens it; a popup shows a 3D item and offers to wear it; items are wardrobe pieces. **The closest thing to a looter loop in the engine** |
| Customisation | cottage menu, wardrobe | recolourable cottage (named heritage paints), window boxes, thatch/slate, character dyes, hair/beard |
| Save | `sv`, key `engtest-1` plus suffixed keys | localStorage JSON: wool, upgrade levels, penned count, happiness, day; separate keys for cottage look, wardrobe, farm name, music, fps, rumble |
| Primer / tips | `tu*`, `toast` | 7-page paged how-to-play; contextual toasts |

### 9.2 Per-frame order (see `loop`, line ~2700)
gamepad poll -> (night: `ngt`) / else: time, weather -> player move, finds, gates, happiness, day -> `shp` (flock) -> waves, streaming -> camera (`camU`) -> per-frame effects (particles, dog, grass trampling, lights, `vwU`) -> render the matching
composer. The frame is capped at 60 fps by default (`fcap`; the loop returns early if called faster than about 10.5 ms).

### 9.3 Hard-wired to the farm (the coupling you must know about)
- The terrain shader's masks (`pathT`, `yardT`, `grassMaskT`, puddle favour) use the **pen, gate, trough, hay, shelter and cottage coordinates**.
- Placement keeps clear of the farm (`farmClear`, `nearFarm`: sheep never spawn within 40 m; "places to search" never within 250 m; graveyards 500 m+). The pen is at the origin.
- The pen fence, gate and the "deposit box" logic are the win condition for the sheep game.
- Sheep, the shepherd's outfit, the crook and the dog are special-cased in several loops (materials, rigs, shadows, NOHIST motion vectors, pools).

## 10. UI and input layer

- **DOM overlay**, not canvas: parchment panels with an SVG knotwork border (`border-image` from data-URI SVG), fonts **Uncial Antiqua** (title), **Metamorphous** (headings), **Fondamento** (body), CSS
  custom properties in `:root` (`--parch`, `--gold`, `--moss`, `--ox`...), a round HUD badge (day dial, weather, sheep count, wool, happiness), a toolbar of round buttons, and an inline **SVG icon sprite** (`<symbol id="i-...">`, referenced with `<use>`).
- Accessibility is real: `prefers-reduced-motion`, `prefers-contrast: more`, `prefers-reduced-transparency`, focus rings (bolder when a pad is in use), 44 pt touch targets on coarse pointers, `aria-*` on dialogs and the loading bar.
- **Touch:** the canvas has a floating virtual joystick (appears where the thumb lands); the crook button is bottom-centre in portrait and moves to the right edge on touch devices in landscape (`body.mob`).
- **Loading:** a staged loading screen ("Waking the hills", "Mixing the paints", "Warming the shaders"...) that **compiles every pipeline before the first frame**, including normally hidden objects shown for one compile pass.
  Any new material or object kind that first appears mid-game will hitch the main thread unless it joins that warm-up.

## 11. Audio (all synthesised, Web Audio)

- SFX are built from noise buffers and oscillators: crook whoosh shaped by the stroke speed, boot-in-puddle splash, gate latch/creak/knock, a trunk's inharmonic chime and shimmer chord, sheep baas, wolf howls, rain.
- **Music** (`mkMusic`, API `MU.tick(level)` / `MU.jingle()`): **gothic darkwave**, a dark 80s synth band with medieval undertones
  (Quest replaced the sheep game's Celtic folk quartet). A new piece each time, in D minor (harmonic minor's A major and a Phrygian
  E flat for the cadences), 84-96 bpm: intro (church bell `bl`, organ open-fifth drone `og`, monks' choir in organum `cr` through a
  formant bank, lute `pl` picking chords), A (shawm tune `sw`, half-time beat), B (synth lead `ld` with the shawm an octave under, full
  beat with a gated snare and tom fill), A, a break (lute alone with tremolo over drone and choir), B, two held bars. Under it: a saw bass
  `bs` and detuned-saw pads `pd` pumping under the kick (`pump`), a pulse arpeggio `ar` into a dotted-eighth echo, and strings, a string
  machine's chorused section (`SB`): a cello `cel` on the roots throughout, violins `str` from the verse's fifth bar, in tremolo with
  the tune doubled in the last chorus. The find's jingle runs up D minor to a Picardy D major with a bell. Still **"played back like a
  32-bit console"**: the lute is 22 kHz Karplus-Strong samples pitched by playback rate, the band is quantised to 11 bits, and it plays
  into a dark hall reverb that updates at 22 kHz. It rests from dusk to dawn as before. Level and CPU were matched to the old quartet by
  offline rendering (about 0.03 RMS, about 0.09 of real time on a desktop). On iOS the audio session follows the silent switch and mixes
  with the player's own music. **N** (not M, which is now the map) or the pause menu switches it.
- Audio starts only after a user gesture (the title's Start button), per browser autoplay rules.

## 12. Performance architecture and hard-won constraints

- **No allocations in the frame loop.** Scratch vectors (`T1..T3`, `C1..C3`, `DM`...) are created once; "build-time only" helpers (`V()`) are never called per frame.
- **Draw-call discipline:** instance pools, shadow tiers, LOD tiers, one-chunk-per-frame building, queued chunk builds. Cost is dominated by full-screen passes, hence the pixel budget.
- **Shader count discipline:** instanced meshes use vertex-attribute matrices (`userData.ia`, a `NodeBuilder` patch at line 448) so a new instance count does not compile a new shader. `customProgramCacheKey` is `key`-based.
- **Motion vectors:** anything posed in the vertex shader needs `NOHIST` (or its own motion node) or TRAA smears it. Pool instances never move by themselves, so their previous-matrix attribute is a clone (`PoolAttr`).
- **Mobile tier (`mob`):** smaller shadow map, fewer mesh segments and grass, lower pixel budget and pixel ratio, fewer foliage cards, DoF bokeh cheaper, fewer AO samples.
- **WebGPU quirks:** 8 vertex buffers per draw; compile on first use unless warmed; `compileAsync` waits a frame per object where `scheduler.yield` is missing (Safari).
- **Texture memory** is a few hundred MB at runtime (render targets plus canvas textures); keep an eye on it.

## 13. Artistic style as it has actually been built (description, not instruction)

These are the choices visible in the code and in the user's repeated feedback during development. Treat them as an inventory of *what the engine already expresses*:

1. **Stylised-natural, not realistic:** soft cel lighting with wrap and translucency, strong rim, warm key and cool fill, painterly split-tone, a slightly desaturated-then-boosted colour curve; heavy use of
   vegetation motion (wind sway, trampled grass, swaying leaf cards).
2. **Miniature / diorama framing:** high three-quarter camera, tilt-shift blur at the top and bottom of frame, haze that swallows the far edge, a vignette. The world feels like a lit tabletop model.
3. **Faceted low-poly for things that stand out** (trees, logs, stumps): each facet one flat colour with baked AO in the creases and crisp colour regions; **smooth shading for architecture and props.**
   The characters (knight, hound, wolves, brigands, skeletons) went further, to **voxel art** (section 18).
4. **Silhouette and colour-region clarity over texture detail:** surface richness comes from procedural bump, vertex colour, painted-on patches (fly-agaric warts, lichen, lime-wash) and *instanced cards*, not image textures.
5. **Authored through observation:** nearly every object is described in a comment from its real-world reference (named tree species, drystone wall courses and coping, a famine cottage's gables, high crosses, church
   bellcotes, heritage paint names, Aran jumper and tweed cap "dressed like the paintings"). The user checked results against reference photos and corrected drift.
6. **Sparse and deliberate rather than dense and random:** the user has repeatedly asked for restraint (ground cover sparse and only where it makes sense, fairy rings rare, graveyards uncommon and far from the farm, fewer lakes
   but a protected one beside the cottage, field hay bales as raw straw scattered like a mown meadow, no red enlarged first letters on headings, no standing stones that look stray).
7. **Everything reacts to the world:** puddles, wet boots, wet fur-less look, rain on the roof, wind direction shared by trees, grass and rain, lamps lit by weather and by the wolves' first howl, smoke drifting downwind.
8. **UI as an object in the world's style:** parchment, knotwork borders, uncial lettering, a day dial; it overlays the 3D scene, un-filtered.
9. **Readability first for gameplay:** the see-through tree fade, the camera clearance logic, shadows that stay put when the canopy fades, sheep that never pop in on screen, black sheep always tucked into bushes.
10. **Sound matches the console idea:** quantised, low-sample-rate folk music in a dark hall.

## 14. Working on the code: practicalities

- **The module has no globals.** To inspect or drive it from a browser console or an automation tool, inject a hook in a *scratch copy* only, for example
  `window.DBG={renderer,sc,cam,P,loop,S,spawn,Hm,capsule,sweep,COLS,THREE,U,...};` immediately before `function loop(){`. Consts defined later need getters.
- **Driving frames manually:** `renderer.setAnimationLoop(null)`, then each frame `renderer.info.reset(); const nf=renderer._nodes.nodeFrame; nf.update(); renderer.info.frame=nf.frameId; DBG.loop();` with at least 12 ms between calls.
  Skipping the `nodeFrame.update()` makes frames silently partial.
- **Automation gotchas:** a hidden browser pane stops `requestAnimationFrame` (load timings are meaningless then); the game **auto-pauses** when the page loses focus (the Paused card; sheep and the player stop but the camera still runs);
  `renderer.domElement.style.opacity` starts at 0 and is raised by the title flow (set it to 1 yourself if you skip the title with synthetic clicks); a viewport narrower than 768 px is treated as a phone.
- **Match the house style.** The code is dense (long lines, short names) and heavily commented with the *why*: comments describe intent, history of bugs, and measurements. New code should read like its neighbours and
  explain non-obvious decisions the same way. Keep edits small and targeted (use exact-match replacements), and never overwrite the whole file blindly: other sessions have edited this file concurrently.
- **Syntax-check without Node:** there may be no Node on the machine; the module can be checked by wrapping its text in `new Function("return async function(){"+code+"}")` under `osascript -l JavaScript`, or just by loading it.
- **Related project pieces:** the original public repo (GitHub Pages site, README, `tools/make-offline.py`); the iOS wrapper `DowraiOS/` (WKWebView, serves its own copy of the game, own git repo).
  This repo (`WIP-HOD-ENGINE-TEST`, private) is an independent copy for the engine experiment: nothing here flows back to those unless someone copies it.

## 18. Voxel characters (the low-poly retro 3D look)

Every character is a **voxel model**: the knight (a base body plus a mesh per piece of armour), the warhound and the wolves, the brigands
and the skeletons. The aim is blocky voxel heroes in the style of the user's references (a big head, a long body, short legs, pixel faces),
detailed enough not to read as Minecraft: half-cell detail, rounded block edges, trims, rivets and shading painted in. There are still no
textures: each cube's colour *is* the texel.

- **`VXS(pieces, bbox, size, opt)`** (after `SCULPT`) cuts SDF primitives (SCULPT's format: `[sdf, colour, blend, material, bone]` plus
  paint) into cubes on one grid: a cell is kept where a piece holds its middle, and the **last piece holding it** colours it (layered as
  SCULPT's `opt.layer`, then the paint), so a later piece's carve (a hood's face hole) never cuts an earlier one (the face behind it).
  Each exposed cell face is a flat square with the cell's colour, a hash grain (`opt.grain`) and darkening by neighbouring cells (AO);
  `opt.tex` patterns a material (the knight's mail checker). Boned figures (`opt.bones`) get bone weights and `aM` **per corner from position
  alone**, so faces meeting at a corner move together and the rig can't tear them; `opt.crisp` / `opt.dye` / `opt.dyeC` bake `PLMm`'s colour
  attributes so `manCol`, `armTint` and the hair dye work unchanged. `opt.skip` leaves cells empty (armour keeps out of the body);
  `userData.occ` reports kept cells. `opt.off` offsets the grid.
- **Cells:** layouts are written in `VXZ` = 0.05 m cells with `CEL(x0,x1,y0,y1,z0,z1,r)` (inclusive cell ranges, `r` rounds edges:
  1 takes a half-cell chamfer, 1.6 rounds a head), `CELS` (mirrored per side) and `CELM`. Every figure is **cut at half cells** (`VXF`,
  `FIG = {off: .5}`), so each layout cell splits into eight and `HPX` / `HPXS` (edges at half-cell precision) add the finer blocks and paint.
- **The knight** (`manG(palette)` returns `{body, h, a, g, l, b, k}`): `BOD(palette)` is the base body (the player under his armour: skin,
  hair, tunic, hose, shoes; `KCL` is the knight's palette, `BCL` the brigand's, so a class is a palette). The armour slots `ARMK = 'hagblk'`
  are separate meshes `ARMM[k]` on the same rig material, shown while `EQ[k]` is worn (`armTint`), each laid a cell out from the body
  (`skip` = body cells) in **its own region** so pieces never overlap: helm the head (rows 44 up), chest the torso front and back plus the
  upper arms (rows 19-43), gloves the forearms and hands (20-33), legs the thighs and shins (4-16), boots the feet (0-3), cloak behind
  (z -6 and out). An armour piece is weighted by **its own blocks' bones** (each tagged with the bone of the part it covers); weighted by the
  body's, plates a cell out from the arm stretched toward the torso. A new piece = a primitive list in its slot's region.
- **New joints** (the knight is ~4.5 heads tall): ankle .12, knee .45, hip .85, waist/skirt pivot 1.4, spine 1.45, elbow 1.7, shoulder
  (±.375, 2.1), neck 2.2, head top ~2.8 (helm 2.9, plume 3.15). `manRig`, `tg` (1.45) / `hd` (.75), the shield hangers, the sword IK
  (`T16`, `IK_S/E/G`), `poseStaff`'s grip targets, the stride (`lp(1.3, 1.7)`), the hip-drop and bob (thigh .4 + shin .33) all use them.
  Arm lengths are as before. Limbs keep **a one-cell gap** from the body and between the legs (cells that touch share corners and their
  weights blend); the crotch block sits under the tunic's skirt and the legs swing whole below the hem. **Rig masks must be crisp** for
  cell figures: soft distance falloffs smeared the legs.
- **Foes** (`FGEO`): `FPT(pieces)` cuts a world-frame figure into the rigid parts `foePose` drives (body from the hips at .85, arms from
  (±.4, 2.1), legs from (±.15, .85); left and right separately), each moved by whole cells so parts line up at rest. A brigand is `BOD(BCL)`
  plus hood, cowl, scarf and sword; a skeleton is `SKB()` (thin bones, hollow ribcage of separate ribs, skull with sunken sockets and teeth)
  plus a rusted cap and a notched blade. The coals (`EYG`) sit in the sockets. Their step phase runs at 2.6 for the shorter legs.
- **The hound** (`dogG`) is laid out on `dogRig`'s joints (legs under x ±.1, z .45 / -.4; all below y 1 is leg, the body above), and
  `dogRig`'s leg mask is now crisp (below the body, fore or aft). **Wolves** (`wolfG`) recolour its cubes to one grey (`WLC`) keeping each
  cube's shade, eyes (aM 2) amber, nose and glints (aM 3) kept.
- **Sword and shield:** the sword is boxes, 1.4 long, grip middle .2 from the pommel (trail `trIn`/`trOut` match); the shield outline is
  stepped in cells.
- **Cost:** the knight about 27k triangles bare and ~81k in full armour, the hound or a wolf ~20k, a brigand ~31k, a skeleton ~20k;
  `manG` takes ~0.3 s at load. Not measured on phones. The per-cell grain makes greedy face merging pointless.
- **Lighting is unchanged:** cel shading (`cel:1`), PCF soft shadows (section 5.3); `BasicShadowMap` would change every shadow in the world.
